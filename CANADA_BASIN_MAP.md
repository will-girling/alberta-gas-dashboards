# Canadian plays and operators — interview reference

Two kinds of number in here, kept separate on purpose:

- **Measured** — Alberta production by licensee, from Petrinex volumetric
  data joined to AER ST37, June 2026. You can defend these.
- **Approximate** — BC, Saskatchewan, offshore, and anything corporate-
  level. From public knowledge, not from your data. Say "roughly" and
  don't quote decimals.

Alberta totals for context: **13.2 Bcf/d gas**, **1.97 MMbbl/d bitumen**,
**550 Mbbl/d conventional crude**. Note the bitumen figure excludes
mined oil sands, which Petrinex withholds — add roughly 1.3 MMbbl/d of
mining for the true provincial number.

## Gas plays

### Montney — the one that matters
Straddles the BC/AB border: Dawson Creek–Groundbirch–Fort St John on the
BC side, Grande Prairie–Kakwa–Wapiti–Pipestone on the AB side.

The thing to know: **it is not one play.** Northeast BC is drier gas;
the Alberta side around Kakwa and Pipestone is liquids-rich, and the
condensate is worth more than the gas. Condensate pricing off Edmonton
is what makes those wells work. If someone says "Montney economics" the
right question is *which part*.

Operators — Tourmaline, ARC (heavily BC: Attachie, Sunrise, Dawson),
Ovintiv, NuVista (Wapiti/Pipestone), Kelt, Paramount, Advantage
(Glacier), Birchcliff (Pouce Coupe), CNRL, and on the BC side Petronas
and Shell tied to LNG Canada.

### Deep Basin / Spirit River / Wilrich
West-central Alberta, tight gas, stacked targets. Lower decline and
lower cost than Montney but less liquids. **Peyto is the pure play** and
the standard reference for low-cost operator. Also CNRL, Vermilion,
Whitecap, Spartan Delta, Ovintiv.

### Duvernay
Liquids-rich shale under the Deep Basin — Kaybob, Fox Creek, Willesden
Green. Long-promised, capital-intensive, never quite scaled the way the
Montney did. Chevron, Ovintiv, CNRL.

### Mannville CBM and shallow gas
Very low rate per well, enormous well count, minimal decline. This is
the long tail in your own data — the 84% of Alberta wells producing
under 0.1 MMcf/d. **Ember Resources** is the pure play and shows 0% of
production from new wells: pure harvest mode, no drilling.

### Horn River / Liard (BC)
Large dry gas resource, largely shelved on cost. Worth knowing it exists
and why nobody drills it.

## Oil plays

### Oil sands mining — Athabasca, north of Fort McMurray
Suncor (Base Plant, Fort Hills), CNRL (Horizon, AOSP/Muskeg River),
Imperial (Kearl), Syncrude (CNRL-operated). Truck-and-shovel plus
upgrading. **Absent from your data** — Petrinex withholds oil sands
facility types.

### Oil sands in-situ (SAGD)
Three areas: Athabasca (Christina Lake, Foster Creek, Firebag, Surmont,
Jackfish, Kirby), Cold Lake (Imperial Cold Lake, CNRL Primrose), Peace

### Clearwater — the one to have a view on
Marten Hills, Peavine, Nipisi. Shallow, cheap, multilateral heavy oil
wells with very high capital efficiency. The most interesting
conventional play in Canada over the last five years.

Operators, visible in your data: **Spur Petroleum** (38.9 Mbbl/d crude
plus 24.8 Mbbl/d bitumen), **Tamarack Valley** (29.9 Mbbl/d),
**Headwater Exploration** (22.9 Mbbl/d). Headwater is the name most
associated with the play.

### Cardium
Light oil, west-central Alberta, mature and well drilled. Whitecap,
Obsidian, InPlay, Cardinal, Aspenleaf, Yangarra.

### Viking
Shallow light oil, east-central AB into southwest Saskatchewan.
Whitecap, and Teine (private) on the Sask side.

### Saskatchewan Bakken / Torquay
Southeast Saskatchewan around Estevan. Whitecap is the main public name
following the Veren acquisition. **Not in your data** — needs the
Petrinex SK pull.

### Lloydminster heavy oil
Straddles the AB/SK border. Conventional and thermal heavy oil. CNRL,
Cenovus, and **Strathcona**, whose Lloydminster Thermal and Conventional
units sit in southwest Saskatchewan and southeast Alberta.

### Offshore Newfoundland
Hibernia, Terra Nova, Hebron, White Rose. Suncor, Cenovus, ExxonMobil.
Small nationally but comes up because it is the only non-western
production of scale.

## Infrastructure — the marketing half of the question

**Gas.** NGTL gathers essentially all Alberta supply and hands off at
Empress (east, to TC Mainline and Saskatchewan) and the Alberta/BC
border (west, via Foothills to Kingsgate). Alliance runs liquids-rich
gas direct to Chicago. Westcoast T-South moves northeast BC gas south to
Huntingdon/Sumas. AECO is the Alberta hub; Station 2 is northeast BC.

LNG Canada at Kitimat, fed by Coastal GasLink, is the structural change
— first material new demand for WCSB gas in decades. Cedar LNG and
Woodfibre follow.

**Oil.** Enbridge Mainline to the US Midwest, Keystone to Cushing and
the Gulf, and TMX to Burnaby — the last of which is what changed the WCS
differential. Heavy oil is priced off WCS at Hardisty; light off
Edmonton Par.

## Two things you can say that most candidates can't

**Alberta gas runs a 25% base decline.** Wells producing a year ago make
3.2 Bcf/d less today; new wells added 3.7 Bcf/d, holding the province
roughly flat at 13.2. Wells under three years old are 82% of supply.

**Horizontal well productivity peaked in 2024.** Measured at the same
month of life, restricted to wells that ever exceed 1 MMcf/d, the 2024
cohort beats 2025 at every age. So the basin is holding production by
drilling more wells, not better ones — which makes supply more sensitive
to a capital pullback than a flat production line suggests.

Both are yours, from well-level data, and both are falsifiable.
